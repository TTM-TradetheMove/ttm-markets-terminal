The top box is the session that most recently went live — when New York's core session starts while London is still open, it takes the top spot and reads "Overlap with London"; once London closes it becomes "Core — solo, no overlap". Before that, New York shows "Pre-market" and London stays on top. After the 16:00 ET close it shows "Post-market". Each box lists its exchanges with their open hours and lunch breaks, and the overlap box counts times when exchanges from two different regions are open at once. Cash markets run Monday–Friday, so everything reads Closed over the weekend and counts down to the next open.

How the score works. 0–100, from relative-strength improvement (30%), breadth expansion (25%, equal-weight vs cap-weight sector ETF), volume accumulation (20%) and macro confirmation (10%). Individual-stock leadership (15%) needs constituent data and is coming next, so the score is scaled over the 85% that is live. Bands: 0–29 weak, 30–49 developing, 50–69 early warning, 70–84 confirming, 85+ strong. Educational research only, not financial advice. Rotation alerts for members stay off until the back-test is proven over a longer history.

Property BETA

The 18-year property cycle

House prices in the US, Australia, the UK, Canada, New Zealand, Germany and Japan, from the BIS database back to the 1970s, with the classic 18-year land cycle laid over the top: peaks around 1973, 1990 and 2007, so the next one fell around 2025. Prices are shown in real terms (after inflation) or nominal, rebased to 100 so countries compare fairly.

Loading property data…

The 18-year cycle is a theory (Homer Hoyt, Fred Foldvary), based on only a few cycles, and every country is different. Governments, rates and population shifts can stretch or break it. Education only, not financial advice.
