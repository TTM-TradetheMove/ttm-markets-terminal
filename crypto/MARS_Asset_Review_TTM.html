What Is Marscoin

The original 2014 Marscoin — not the BSC meme token or other similarly named tokens — and what it is actually for.

The Basics

  • Marscoin is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency launched in January 2014 by Lennart Lopin, a software architect and Mars Society member, with James Burk listed as co-founder. It markets itself as the first cryptocurrency dedicated to supporting Mars colonization.
  • The whitepaper argues that 4 to 24 minute Earth–Mars communication delays make conventional banking impractical, so the coin was designed to work without a central authority on Earth.
  • Per Bitrue and Bittime explainers, it uses Scrypt proof-of-work with roughly 2-minute blocks. One tracker (CoinLore) lists the algorithm as X11 instead — sources disagree, so verify against the project's own documentation.
  • Fixed supply of 39,569,900 MARS (some trackers round to 40M).
  • Name-collision warning: an unrelated BSC meme token ($MARSCOIN, 1B supply) and other small tokens share the name. Aster DEX perpetuals announced in Aug 2026 were for the BSC meme token, not this coin.

What the token does

  • Peer-to-peer currency — the coin is a payments-style asset with no smart-contract ecosystem or DeFi layer identified.
  • Mining security — block rewards pay miners. AuxPoW merged mining (reported as activated in 2025) lets miners secure Marscoin using hash power from Litecoin and other Scrypt chains.
  • Martian Republic layer — a citizen-registration and voting concept using CoinShuffle for voter privacy and IPFS for resource records, per Bittime.
  • Symbolic donations — 500,000 MARS went to the Mars Society at launch, and Bittime reports a further 500,000 from Mars One in 2019.
  • No staking, fee burn or buyback mechanism was found.

Catalysts & Risks

Potential Catalysts

  • Active maintenance — Core 28.1.2 (Apr 2026), ASERT (2024) and AuxPoW merged mining (2025) show the codebase is still being updated.
  • Merged-mining security — sharing Scrypt hash power with Litecoin could improve network security without extra issuance.
  • Named, traceable team and a USPTO trademark (2023) give it more legitimacy than most novelty coins.
  • Mars/space headlines historically drove its 2021 spike, though it has no connection to SpaceX or Elon Musk.
  • A 1-year gain of about +37% (CoinLore) shows price can move sharply on tiny volume.

Key Risks

  • Extreme illiquidity — roughly $10K daily volume, one exchange (LBank, MARS/USDC). Slippage can be severe and exits difficult.
  • No verified circulating supply, so market cap and valuation cannot be reliably measured.
  • Name confusion — BSC and Solana tokens with similar names exist; wrong-token purchases and contaminated data are realistic hazards. Verify via the original chain/explorer.
  • About 98% below its Feb 2021 high, with no identified usage beyond symbolic donations.
  • Weak financial transparency and no recent full independent audit found (per Bittime); entity naming (Inc. vs LLC) is inconsistent.
