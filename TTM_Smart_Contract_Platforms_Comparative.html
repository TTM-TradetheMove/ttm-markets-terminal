Smart Contract Platform Tokens

General-purpose L1s and Ethereum L2s/appchains — where each sits on TVL, market structure, and real on-chain usage.
Educational research report · Data sourced from DeFiLlama / L2BEAT / CoinGecko · Compiled September 2026
Scope. "Smart contract platform" here means any chain or rollup where developers can deploy arbitrary contracts — not just move value. That includes monolithic L1s (Ethereum, Solana) and Ethereum-anchored L2s/appchains (Arbitrum, Starknet, Immutable). Figures below are approximate and move fast — TVL and market cap in crypto can shift double digits in a week, so treat these as a snapshot, not a fixed ranking.

Ethereum L2s & Appchains

Rollups and appchains that settle to Ethereum for security while executing off it. TVL gaps between these are large — from billions down to tens of millions.

Optimism

Spotlight — Where does IMX actually rank?

IMX is a genuine smart contract platform — the unified Immutable Chain supports full EVM contracts (the original Immutable X rollup didn't; it only supported pre-defined APIs). But it's a narrow, single-vertical platform (gaming/NFTs), not a general-purpose competitor to Ethereum or Solana, and its on-chain numbers reflect that.

The honest read: that 30-day fee number is not a typo — it's genuinely near-zero relative to its market cap, despite Immutable's "200+ games committed" narrative. This is the same pattern flagged elsewhere in TTM research (Hedera's near-zero protocol revenue, AVAX's ecosystem-growth-≠-token-value problem): a platform can have real infrastructure and real partnerships without that translating into measurable on-chain fee activity or token value capture. IMX belongs on a smart-contract-platform list on technical grounds; it does not currently belong on a revenue-generation list.
